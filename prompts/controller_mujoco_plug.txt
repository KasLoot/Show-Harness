Images are ordered Wrist (A), Front (B), Right Side (C), Angled Wrist (D).
These are four RGB views.
The CURRENT A-D set comes first, with its matching WRIST_DEPTH_MM text array and
calibration. Any older sets are labeled HISTORY step N and follow oldest to newest.
Each history set includes the depth array and calibration from that SAME before-action
observation, paired with the executed action and measured movement that followed it.
Use history to see how a correction changed alignment or stalled, not
as evidence that an old alignment still holds. CURRENT images govern the next action
and stage completion. A commanded movement is not proof that it occurred. Do not reuse
old wrist pixel coordinates after the hand camera moves or rotates.
Translation axes remain fixed in the robot/world frame after every rotation.
Both external cameras are orthographic, tilted 30 degrees downward toward the table.
GRIPPER motion in Front (B), on the +X side:
- MV_FWD (+X): closer to the camera. MV_BACK (-X): farther from the camera.
- MV_RIGHT (+Y): image-right. MV_LEFT (-Y): image-left.
GRIPPER motion in Right Side (C), on the +Y side:
- MV_FWD (+X): image-left. MV_BACK (-X): image-right.
- MV_RIGHT (+Y): closer to the camera. MV_LEFT (-Y): farther from the camera.
MV_UP (+Z) moves vertically upward, away from the table;
MV_DOWN (-Z) moves vertically downward, toward the table.
Front's horizontal position isolates Y alignment; Right Side's isolates X alignment.
Depth and height can overlap visually; cross-check the views. Orthographic depth does
not change apparent size. Image overlap alone does not establish alignment.
Both wrist cameras move and rotate with the hand; their image axes are NOT fixed
world directions. Use each camera's current measured image-right, image-down, and
available sightline world vectors supplied below to select world-axis movements.
A / Wrist: its EXACT image center is the grasp point (TCP). During pre-grasp approach,
use that point as the handle-centering anchor in BOTH image axes. An object anywhere
between widely open fingers is not necessarily centered.
D / Angled Wrist: this oblique camera looks below the TCP to expose the silver pin
and black bore rim. Its image center is NOT the grasp point or a centering target.
The WRIST_DEPTH_MM observation's depth_mm text array is point-sampled from the SAME
camera and projection as A, normally 32 rows by 32 columns. Rows run top to bottom
and columns left to right. source_width_px/source_height_px describe the original
RGB dimensions; sample_y_px/sample_x_px map each array row/column to exact zero-based
pixel indices in A. An entry is one sampled pixel, not an average over a tile. Use this
mapping and A to identify visible handle and fingertip-pad surfaces before comparing
their depths. Values are camera-axis distance in MILLIMETRES, rounded to 1 mm, NOT
world Z or clearance from the TCP. Use the supplied range and camera-to-TCP calibration;
the camera is behind the grasp point. Wrist rotation changes the relationship between
camera-axis depth and world Z. null means invalid or outside the configured range,
never proof of empty space. Only visible, sampled surfaces have depth evidence; the
sparse grid can miss narrow edges or pads and does not provide depth for every RGB
pixel. A number alone cannot identify an object or establish its complete shape,
pose, or contact. There is no fifth image; local grayscale diagnostics are not model inputs.
In either wrist view, camera translation parallel to its image plane makes a stationary
target shift oppositely in the image; motion along its sightline mainly changes apparent size.
Use the external views to bring objects into view and check height. Rotations turn
the hand about its grasp point and carry the grasped plug with it; translations preserve
orientation. The silver pin and black bore are round: no keyed yaw alignment is needed.

GRASP AND CONTACT:
{gripper_proprio}
- During GRASP/pre-grasp approach only, before lowering toward the handle or closing,
  establish XY alignment with independent positive evidence from A, B, and C:
  A / Wrist: the orange handle center must be centered at the EXACT image center in
  BOTH axes. A cropped or edge-only handle view cannot confirm alignment.
  B / Front: compare the handle center horizontally with the midpoint of the inner
  {gripper_color} fingertip pads, not the hand body. Target left -> MV_LEFT;
  target right -> MV_RIGHT. This checks Y alignment.
  C / Right Side: compare the same handle center horizontally with the grasp-point /
  inner fingertip-pad midpoint column. Target left -> MV_FWD; target right -> MV_BACK.
  This checks X alignment. Visible Side offset vetoes descent and GRASP even if A looks aligned.
  D adds useful grasp evidence when visible; full handle visibility in D is not required.
- Before closing, compare mapped handle and fingertip-pad samples in WRIST_DEPTH_MM against
  their identities and geometry in A/B/C. Depth must be consistent with the pads reaching
  around the handle body. A shallow fingertip touch or edge-only grip is insufficient.
  Similar numerical depths do not prove secure contact, and different visible surfaces
  need not have identical values. The array supplements the independent A/B/C alignment
  checks; it does not reveal an occluded or unsampled pad or handle surface.
- The handle is 32 mm tall. When it is upright and A looks straight down, its side-body
  center is 16 mm below its visible top face. For a body-center grasp, the top-face
  depth should therefore be approximately 16 mm CLOSER to the camera than the supplied
  TCP-plane depth, not equal to it. Cross-check A/B/C and the visible finger-pad depths.
  Do not apply this shortcut to a tilted handle/wrist or an obscured top face.
- Align XY at safe clearance, then approach handle height with SMALL steps near it,
  rechecking after every action. GRASP requires the actual fingertip pads to straddle
  the handle body at contact height; being above it or overlapping its silhouette is
  insufficient. Grasp the body, not an edge.
- Reported height above the table is not clearance above the handle; images establish
  actual contact height. These pre-grasp centering checks do not apply to INSERT/PLACE:
  there align the held pin with the bore, not the handle or TCP with an image center.
- These grasp checks override generic rough-alignment, table-height, and oscillation-based
  descent hints. Uncertain evidence means no GRASP. Make a SMALL correction when visually
  justified; otherwise STOP. Do not use DONE or new subgoals to escape an unresolved grasp.
- An empty close establishes neither "too high" nor "overshot". Re-evaluate X, Y, and Z
  independently in fresh images before choosing the next correction.
- Nonzero gripper width alone does not prove a centered, secure grasp. Edge contact cannot
  satisfy grasp-stage DONE. Visually verify secure handle contact before lift/transport.
- During GRASP, RELEASE may reopen a visibly off-center or edge grasp only while the
  plug remains supported on its source stand/surface; then realign before closing.
  Otherwise RELEASE only when the plug is seated and supported at its destination.

LIFT BEFORE TRANSPORT:
- After a secure handle-body grasp, complete a separate 50 mm (5 cm) upward lift
  before any lateral transport toward the tall blue socket. Choose MV_UP_LARGE,
  which commands exactly 50 mm in the default plug configuration, as one atomic decision.
  Use the next observation and measured movement to verify completion; issuing the
  token alone does not complete the lift. If motion was incomplete, inspect the cause
  and finish the remaining lift with justified sized upward steps rather than blindly
  repeating the full 50 mm command.
  If command sizes were customized, use their supplied amounts to complete the same lift goal.
- LIFT DONE requires the measured 50 mm upward movement from the secure-grasp pose,
  secure carry with no handle slip, and the pin bottom visibly clear of the stand
  and the blue socket's top along the transport path. If clearance is still inadequate,
  raise safely before lateral travel. A TCP rise without plug rise is not a successful lift.
- During transport or later stages, do not restart the completed post-grasp LIFT milestone
  simply because a stage or recovery begins. Use the current stage and available history
  to interpret progress; a recovery that requires a new secure grasp requires a new lift.
  Subsequent clearance corrections depend on current images. This remains a VLM-chosen action milestone,
  not an automatic lift or a multi-action response.

PIN INSERTION:
- The orange handle carries a silver cylindrical pin, 20 mm in diameter. The blue socket
  contains an open black cylindrical bore, nominally 26 mm wide and 50 mm deep.
  The black region is the bore interior, not a solid cap or a surface to place the plug on.
- Complete the 50 mm lift and verify transport clearance first. Align the actual silver pin axis/tip
  with the BLACK bore rim, not the orange handle center or the blue housing center.
  Front checks lateral Y alignment; Right Side checks X alignment. D reveals the rim,
  tip, lateral offset, and shaft tilt. Compare the pin centerline with the actual opening
  in these independent views; plausible alignment in D cannot overrule visible offset
  in Front or Right Side. The bore has only about 3 mm radial clearance around the pin.
- In D, a hovering tip and the hole have different heights and therefore oblique-view
  parallax: do not demand coincident tip/hole pixels. Extrapolate the shaft axis to the
  rim plane and center it in the opening, with clearance around the full pin outline,
  cross-checking Front and Right Side. Partial silhouette overlap is not a fit.
  At final approach, a pin edge clipping one rim side while the bore remains visible
  on the other is evidence of possible offset, not insertion. Assess this asymmetry
  using the rim plane and orthogonal views; oblique perspective does not require equal
  pixel gaps. If alignment uncertainty is comparable to the 3 mm radial clearance,
  restore an inspectable view and refine alignment before descending or declaring DONE.
- Finish transport/alignment only when CURRENT views support this pin-axis/rim check.
  A new stage, a previous DONE, or alignment with the housing does not establish entry
  alignment. At the final approach and immediately before each downward step, recheck
  D plus Front/Right Side; an earlier coarse alignment may no longer be sufficient.
- Before entry, visible lateral offset or an unlocalized rim vetoes MV_DOWN and DONE
  for transport/alignment or insertion. While the tip is clear above the rim, make a
  SMALL lateral correction when its direction is justified. If the hand or pin hides
  the rim, use a safe SMALL upward move to restore clearance and inspect, then realign.
  A previously visible rim may help locate it against CURRENT fixed-camera landmarks;
  it cannot override current offset or replace the final check. Use STOP only when no
  correction or clearance move is justified, rather than repeating it despite a safe lift.
- Choose LARGE only for clear travel, MEDIUM for approach, and SMALL near the rim/contact.
  Correct translation and tilt in separate decisions, inspecting fresh images each time.
  Once entry is visibly established, rim occlusion is expected: use current external
  views, measured pose, and actual motion to judge insertion depth. Occlusion alone
  does not require repeated withdrawal. A commanded descent or silhouette overlap is
  not evidence of entry or seating. If motion stalls, lateral offset appears, or contact
  blocks insertion, lift clear before lateral correction; do not slide against the rim,
  keep pushing, or rotate inside the bore.
- Use HISTORY-to-CURRENT comparisons to track the pin tip relative to the rim and the
  handle relative to the fingertip pads. Successful TCP descent does NOT prove that
  the plug descended. If the pin stays on the rim while the hand lowers, or the handle
  shifts upward between the fingers, the plug is blocked/slipping: stop descent, lift
  for clearance when safe, verify or re-establish secure handle contact, and realign.
  Do not compensate for slippage with more downward steps or claim insertion from hand motion.
- A plug hovering over the opening or resting on its rim is not inserted. Complete insertion
  only when the pin is seated at insertion depth and the plug is supported by the socket.
  Confirm visible seating before RELEASE, using external depth/seating cues when the rim
  is occluded. A pin tip still above the rim is hovering. Do not release in the expectation
  that gravity will finish insertion.
  Release at the destination only after seating, then complete the distinct RETREAT stage below.

RETREAT AFTER RELEASE:
- RETREAT is the final withdrawal of the OPEN, empty hand from the seated plug. It is
  distinct from the initial LIFT while carrying the plug. After release, choose one
  MV_UP_LARGE (50 mm by default), then inspect a fresh observation before another action.
- RETREAT DONE requires open fingers, the plug still seated and undisturbed, and the
  TCP strictly MORE THAN {retreat_clearance_mm} mm vertically above the visible top of
  the orange handle; aim for about {retreat_target_mm} mm to leave a margin, as specified
  by the completion-clearance block. A merely visible gap or 50 mm of commanded hand
  travel is insufficient: the starting TCP can be below the handle top. If clearance
  remains short, choose an appropriate upward step and inspect again before DONE.
- Judge the handle top from CURRENT images and mapped depth samples, not assumed
  object coordinates. With Wrist looking straight down, sampled top-face depth minus
  the calibrated camera-to-TCP depth estimates the TCP's vertical gap above that top.
  Use the visible 32 mm-tall handle as an additional scale cue and cross-check Front
  and Right Side. Do not apply the depth subtraction as world height with a tilted
  wrist or to a hidden/unsampled top; use the current camera basis and views instead.
  Measured TCP motion alone does not prove that the plug stayed seated.
