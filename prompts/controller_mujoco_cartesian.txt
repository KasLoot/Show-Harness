DIRECTION:
Images are ordered Wrist (A), Front (B), Right Side (C).
Translation axes remain fixed in the robot/world frame after every rotation.
Both external cameras are orthographic, tilted 30 degrees downward toward the table.
GRIPPER motion in Front (B), on the +X side:
- MV_FWD (+X): closer to the camera. MV_BACK (-X): farther from the camera.
- MV_RIGHT (+Y): image-right. MV_LEFT (-Y): image-left.
GRIPPER motion in Right Side (C), on the +Y side:
- MV_FWD (+X): image-left. MV_BACK (-X): image-right.
- MV_RIGHT (+Y): closer to the camera. MV_LEFT (-Y): farther from the camera.
MV_UP (+Z) moves vertically upward, away from the table;
MV_DOWN (-Z) moves vertically downward, toward the table.
Front's horizontal position isolates Y alignment; Right Side's isolates X alignment.
Depth and height can overlap visually; cross-check the external views and Wrist.
Orthographic depth does not change apparent size. Image overlap alone does not
establish alignment. Being above the target requires table-plane alignment and clearance.
The wrist camera moves and rotates with the hand; its image left/right/up/down are
NOT fixed world directions. Its EXACT image center is the grasp point (TCP); during
pre-grasp approach, use that point as the centering anchor in BOTH image axes.
An object anywhere between widely
open fingers is not necessarily centered. Use the current measured wrist image-right and
image-down world vectors supplied below to choose the corresponding world-axis command.
Camera translation parallel to the Wrist image plane makes a stationary target shift
oppositely in the image. Motion along the Wrist line of sight mainly changes apparent size.
Use Front and Right Side to bring a target that is outside Wrist into view and to check height.
Rotations turn the hand about its grasp point and carry a grasped object with it.
They do not move the grasp point to a new position. Translations preserve orientation.

PRECISION AND CONTACT:
- Choose LARGE only for clear travel; MEDIUM for approach; SMALL near objects or contact.
- Correct position and orientation in separate decisions, checking fresh images each time.
- Match the held object's required orientation to its destination before descending.
- Compare the held object's contact point with the destination, not only the fingertips.
- If descent stalls or the object touches an obstruction, back out and re-align using small steps.
- If corrections overshoot repeatedly, reduce the step size and inspect all three views.
- Do not infer successful placement from overlap in one view or from a gripper close alone.

  B / Front: compare the graspable part's center horizontally with the midpoint of the

  C / Right Side: compare the same target center horizontally with the grasp-point /

  This checks X alignment. Visible Side offset vetoes descent and GRASP even if Wrist looks aligned.
- Align XY at safe clearance, then approach the graspable part's height with SMALL
  steps near it, rechecking all views after each action. GRASP requires the actual
  fingertip pads to straddle its body at contact height; being above it or overlapping

- Reported height above the table is not clearance above the handle; use the images
  to establish actual contact height. These pre-grasp centering checks do not apply
  to INSERT/PLACE: there align the held object's contact point with its destination,
  which may be occluded, rather than requiring it to coincide with the TCP.
- These checks override generic rough-alignment, table-height, and oscillation-based
  descent hints. Uncertain evidence means no GRASP. Make a SMALL correction when a
  direction is visually justified; otherwise STOP. Do not use DONE or new subgoals
  to escape an unresolved grasp.
- An empty close establishes neither "too high" nor "overshot". Re-evaluate X, Y,
  and Z independently in the fresh images before choosing the next correction.
- Nonzero gripper width alone does not prove a centered, secure grasp. Edge contact
  cannot satisfy grasp-stage DONE. Visually verify secure contact around the graspable
  part's body before declaring the grasp complete or starting lift/transport.
- During GRASP, RELEASE may reopen a visibly off-center or edge grasp only while the
  object remains supported on its source stand/surface; then realign before closing.
  Otherwise RELEASE only when the object is supported at its destination.
